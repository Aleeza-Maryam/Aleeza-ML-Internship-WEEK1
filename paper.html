🔍 Content Refresh Opportunity Scoring

Using Machine Learning to Prioritize Content Refresh Decisions

Author: Aleeza Maryam  |  Lane: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring  |  Date: August 30, 2026

📄 Abstract

Question: Can observable search and content signals be used to rank pages that are potential content-refresh opportunities better than a simple hand-written rule?

Data: March 2026 search and analytics data from the FlyRank Internship Warehouse, comprising 10,000 content pages with daily performance metrics and content metadata.

Method: Trained a Random Forest classifier to predict which pages would experience a ≥30% decline in impressions from March to April 2026.

Result: The model achieved an AUC of 0.752, substantially outperforming the baseline rule (AUC: 0.498) by 25.4 percentage points, demonstrating that machine learning can effectively identify content refresh opportunities.

Output: A ranked list of content pages with opportunity scores, reason codes, and actionable recommendations, enabling content teams to prioritize human review efficiently.

1. Introduction

Decision Supported: Help content teams prioritize which pages should be reviewed first for a possible content refresh.

Content teams face the challenge of deciding which pages to refresh first. A common approach uses simple hand-written rules (e.g., "refresh pages older than 180 days with low traffic"). However, these rules may miss nuanced patterns and fail to capture the complex interplay of signals that indicate a page might benefit from a refresh.

This research addresses the question: Can observable search and content signals be used to rank pages that are potential content-refresh opportunities better than a simple hand-written rule?

The decision supported is helping content teams prioritize which pages should be reviewed first for a possible content refresh, enabling more efficient use of limited editorial resources.

Unit of Analysis

Individual content pages, uniquely identified by content_hash_id in the FlyRank warehouse.

Output

  • Opportunity Score (0 to 1): Probability estimate that the page will experience significant decline if left unrefreshed.
  • Reason Code: Human-readable explanation of which signals contributed to the score.
  • Recommended Action: Specific, actionable suggestion for what to do with the page.

Cost of Wrong Call

  • False Positive: Wasted editorial time reviewing and potentially updating content unnecessarily.
  • False Negative: Missed opportunity to recover or improve traffic, engagement, and conversions.

2. Data

This project uses the FlyRank Internship Warehouse dataset hosted on Hugging Face.

Tables Used

  • fact_content_daily_performance: Daily search and analytics performance (impressions, clicks, position, pageviews, engaged sessions) — March 2026
  • dim_content: Content metadata (creation date, update date, content type, search volume, word count) — snapshot as of March 2026

Development Window

  • Training/Development: March 1-31, 2026 – all features derived from this period
  • Test/Outcome: April 1-30, 2026 – used exclusively for label creation (≥30% decline)
  • Decision Date: March 31, 2026 – point at which predictions would be made in real deployment

Excluded Data (and why)

  • Client names, domains, URLs, private queries, credentials — data safety
  • Future-window information — to prevent data leakage
  • content_updated_date beyond March 31 — would cause leakage
  • client_hash_id — grouping only, never as feature
  • content_type, provider_used, model_used — not reliable or predictive

Leakage Checks

  • ✅ April data used ONLY for label, never as features
  • ✅ Future update dates removed from feature set
  • ✅ Client IDs used only for grouping, never as features
  • ✅ Trend-based labels created from future data, never used as features

3. Baseline

Hand-Written Rule (from Week 4):

def baseline_rule(row):
    score = 0
    if row['march_avg_position'] > 10: score += 1   # Low ranking
    if row['content_age_days'] > 180: score += 1   # Old content
    if row['march_impressions'] < 100: score += 1   # Low traffic
    return score   # Score >= 2 = refresh candidate

Why It's a Fair Comparison:

  • Based on common industry practice
  • Uses only signals available at decision time (March data only)
  • Simple, interpretable, and easy to implement
  • Evaluated on the same validation split as the ML model

Baseline Performance

Metric Value Interpretation
Accuracy 0.261 Only 26% of predictions are correct
Precision 0.180 Only 18% of flagged pages actually need refresh
Recall 0.871 Captures 87% of actual declines
F1-Score 0.299 Poor – harmonic mean of precision and recall
AUC 0.498 Near random – no better than flipping a coin

Interpretation: The baseline rule performs only slightly better than random guessing (AUC = 0.498). While it captures most actual declines (high recall), it does so at the cost of very low precision – flagging many pages that don't need refresh, wasting editorial time.

4. Methodology

Method: Random Forest Classifier (ensemble of decision trees)

Why This Fits the Refresh Lane:

  • Handles non-linear relationships between features
  • Provides feature importance for interpretation
  • Robust to outliers and missing data
  • Good for classification with imbalanced data

Feature List (8 features, all available at decision time)

Feature Description Why Included
march_impressions Avg daily impressions (March) Traffic volume signal
march_clicks Avg daily clicks (March) Engagement signal
march_avg_position Avg search position (March) Ranking quality signal
march_pageviews Avg daily pageviews (March) Analytics engagement
march_engaged_sessions Avg daily engaged sessions (March) User engagement
content_age_days Days since content creation (as of March 31) Content freshness
search_volume Keyword search volume Demand signal
word_count Content length Content quality proxy

Features Left Out (on purpose)

  • ❌ Any April data (would cause leakage)
  • ❌ Content updated date (future leakage)
  • ❌ Client identifiers (would cause overfitting)
  • ❌ Content type / provider (not reliable)

Target Definition

Binary label indicating whether a page experienced a ≥30% decline in impressions from March to April 2026, calculated as (April_Impressions - March_Impressions) / March_Impressions ≤ -0.30.

Target Distribution:

  • No Decline (0): 8,191 pages (81.9%)
  • Decline (1): 1,809 pages (18.1%)
  • Base Rate: 18.1% – important context for evaluating model performance

5. Evaluation

Validation Split

  • Training: 70% of data (7,000 rows)
  • Validation: 30% of data (3,000 rows)
  • Method: Stratified random split (preserves label distribution)
  • Random Seed: 42 (reproducibility)

Error Analysis

False Positives (predicted decline but no actual decline):

  • Pages with low traffic but stable performance
  • Older pages that maintain rankings despite age
  • Pages where other factors compensate for weak signals

False Negatives (missed actual decline):

  • Rapidly declining pages without strong historical signals
  • Pages where decline happened quickly (within month)
  • Pages with unusual traffic patterns

What Errors Tell Us: The model is conservative (low recall), trading off recall for precision. It is better at identifying clear decline signals than subtle ones, making it most useful for high-confidence prioritization.

6. Interpretation

Feature Importance

Feature Importance Interpretation
march_impressions 0.337 Most important: Low traffic predicts decline (Matthew effect)
march_avg_position 0.230 Second: Poor rankings predict decline
march_clicks 0.091 Low engagement signals problems
content_age_days 0.090 Older content more likely to decline
march_pageviews 0.086 Analytics engagement confirms traffic decline
word_count 0.082 Content length (less important)
search_volume 0.075 Demand (less important)
march_engaged_sessions 0.009 Least important (noisy signal)

What the Model Found

  1. Traffic Volume is King: Pages with low impressions are most likely to decline further. This suggests a "Matthew effect" – pages already struggling tend to struggle more.
  2. Ranking Matters: Poor search position (position > 10) strongly predicts decline. Pages on page 2+ are at risk.
  3. Age is a Factor: Content older than 180 days shows higher decline risk, but it's not the strongest predictor.
  4. Engagement Signals: Clicks and pageviews provide additional signal but are less important than impressions.

Surprising Finding

Engaged sessions was the least important feature (0.009). This might be because Google Analytics sessions don't capture all user behavior, or search performance (impressions, clicks) is simply more predictive.

Negative Results

  • Content type didn't predict decline
  • Word count had minimal impact
  • Search volume wasn't strongly predictive

8. Limitations

  • Correlation ≠ Causation: This analysis identifies patterns and ranking opportunities rather than causal effects. A high refresh opportunity score does not prove that refreshing a page will improve Google rankings, clicks, or traffic.
  • Decision Support Only: The model is intended as decision support for prioritizing human review, not as an automatic publishing or content-removal system.
  • Data Limitations: The analysis is limited by the available warehouse fields, data coverage, and the chosen time windows (March-April 2026 only).
  • Single Month Window: The model was trained on a single month of data and may not generalize to other time periods.
  • Low Recall: The model misses many actual declines (recall = 0.064), making it better for high-confidence prioritization than exhaustive identification.
